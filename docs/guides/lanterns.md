# Lanterns

Hanging lanterns, lamps and torches are no longer just decoration: you can take them down, carry them, light your way and throw them.

## Which Lanterns Can Be Picked Up

- **Small Lantern**: the most common lantern on a chain, found in the Mines, Coal Pits and most other biomes.
- **Lantern**: a larger glass lantern with a stronger light, found in liquid caves and towns.
- **Mining Lamp**: a small oil lamp in a wooden frame, found on the surface: in the Hills, near the lake and in the snowy wasteland.
- **Tube Lamp**: a fluorescent glass tube with a cold white light, found in the Hiisi Base, the Vault and the Power Plant. It shines only while it hangs on its power cable: once you take it down or knock it off, it goes dark. A lamp you take down drags its cable from the ceiling and keeps shining until the cable runs out and snaps. A lamp you drop or throw on its cable is held back by it. A lamp knocked off by a shot or a kick dangles by one end on a short cable, swings and blinks. Its inventory icon shows whether it still has power. The torn cable keeps hanging and, after a couple of seconds, sparks from its end for a few minutes, shocking every creature close to it: keep away from it, or lure enemies to it. You hold it by its end like a sword. It does not burn: when it breaks, it bursts like a small gas cylinder instead of spilling burning oil. The left mouse button throws it like the right one: it does not hang back on the ceiling.

- **Temple Lantern**: a lantern of the Holy Mountain that glows with blue plasma, found in the Holy Mountains and some secret chambers. It hangs on a long chain that swings when you bump into it. A shot can break the chain, and the lantern falls together with the lower part of it. When the lantern breaks, it bursts and spills fading plasma instead of burning oil.
- **Chain Torch**: a hanging iron brazier of glowing coals on a short chain. It comes in three colours: the plain one, the blue one and the ghostly green one. Its fire is open, see [Chain Torches](#chain-torches).

Torch stands, burning barrels and other light sources stay in place, but they also dispel the darkness around them. See [Vision and Darkness](vision-and-darkness.md).

## Picking Up

Walk up to a hanging or fallen lantern and press the interaction key (`E` by default).

- A lantern takes one Quick Inventory slot, the same slots used by flasks and other items. Lanterns do not stack.
- If your inventory is full, nothing happens.
- You cannot drink the oil inside a lantern.
- Picked-up lanterns do not come back when you leave the area and return.

## Holding a Lantern

Select a lantern in your inventory to hold it in your hand.

- It lights up the area around you in every direction and clears the fog of war.
- It swings as you walk and fly, and settles when you stop.
- A lantern in your hand is safe: it never sets you, wooden structures, gases or liquids on fire, and it does not break while you hold it. Chain torches are the exception, see [Chain Torches](#chain-torches).

## Lantern on Your Belt

When your hands are busy with a wand, a flask or another item, the first lantern in your Quick Inventory hangs on your belt.

- It gives off a softer light than a lantern in hand. You can change its brightness, or turn it off, with the `Belt lantern light` setting.
- It sways with your movement.
- As soon as you take any lantern in hand, the belt lantern disappears.
- A lantern that burns out on your belt shows as a dark lantern and gives no light.

## Chain Torches

A chain torch burns with an open fire, unlike the lanterns behind glass.

- Its sparks fly in the world, in your hand and on your belt.
- In your hand, some of its sparks are real fire: they set flammable materials around you alight. On your belt, its fire is only for show.
- A torch goes out when its coals get into water or another liquid. It gives no light then.
- A torch that went out lights again from fire: in the world when it catches fire, in your hand when you are burning.
- A chain torch is very sturdy. Falls and throws only dent it, but shots and explosions break it, and it scatters fire around.

## Throwing

Throw a held lantern with the right mouse button. It flies along the dotted aiming line like any other thrown item.

The left mouse button does not throw a held lantern. It hangs the lantern on the ceiling right above your head, and your hands take the first wand from your Quick Inventory, or the first item if you carry no wands. A lantern hung this way behaves like any other hanging lantern: you can pick it up again, kick it down, and it falls when the ceiling is destroyed.

> [!NOTE]
> With no ceiling just above your head, the left mouse button keeps the lantern in your hand. The throw animation and sound still play, as the game does not allow canceling the throw.

- A thrown lantern keeps burning where it lands.
- A hard hit shatters it: it explodes, spills burning oil and sets the area on fire. Enemies it hits on the way take damage.
- After a soft landing it stays intact, and you can pick it up or kick it again.

## Knocking Lanterns Down

- **Kick** a hanging lantern to tear it off the ceiling.
- **Destroy the ceiling** above a lantern, and it falls down.
- **Shoot the chain** of a temple lantern or a chain torch, and the lantern falls with the lower part of the chain.

## Luring Fire Creatures

Fire creatures, such as Stendari and Liekkiö, are drawn to a lit lantern hanging, lying or thrown in the world. Once they see it, they gather around it and stay close.

- They do not attack the lantern.
- The light holds them in a trance: they notice you only when you come close, and wake up when you hurt them. Sneak past them, or strike first and fight them one by one.
- A creature that is already fighting you is not lured.
- A lantern in your hands, inventory or on your belt does not lure them.
- Throw or hang a lantern to lead fire creatures away, or to gather them in one place.
- Once a creature is lured, it stays near the lantern even if it loses sight of it for a moment.
- The `Lanterns attract fire enemies` setting turns this off. See [Settings](settings.md#lanterns-attract-fire-enemies).
- With the `Natural Fear` setting, Hiisi and insects are lured the same way. See [Settings](settings.md#natural-fear).

## Scaring Creatures

With the `Natural Fear` setting, the creatures that lanterns do not lure, such as zombies and Limanuljaska, fear lantern light. See [Settings](settings.md#natural-fear).

- The light scorches them: a creature in it sparkles as if confused, panics as if it were on fire and backs away from the light. It takes no real damage.
- Creatures immune to fire, such as ghosts and robots, do not fear the light.
- The lantern in your hands scares them too, so melee creatures keep their distance. Ranged ones still shoot from the darkness. A lantern on your belt or in your inventory does not scare them.
- They fear the light even while fighting you.
- Now and then a scared creature flies into a rage (it looks berserk) and attacks the lantern it feared, or you if the lantern is in your hands. The rage passes after a few seconds. An enraged creature can break a lantern, and the darkness spreads.
- Once the light is gone, they come back home.

## Studying Enemies

With the `Enemy study` setting, you learn how to fight an enemy by watching it in lantern light. See [Settings](settings.md#enemy-study).

- You study an enemy while it is close to you, you can see it, and it stands in the light of a lantern. The lantern may hang or lie in the world, or be in your hands. A projectile with the Light spell modifier also lights enemies for study. The lantern on your belt does not count.
- The longer you study an enemy, the more damage you deal to it, up to the bonus you chose in the settings.
- The enemy keeps its bonus when it leaves the light or your sight. Its study simply pauses.
- A small dot above the enemy shows its study. The dot is yellow while you study the enemy, red while the study is paused, and green once the enemy is fully studied.
- You learn about enemy types, not only single enemies. Each enemy you study makes the next ones of the same type faster to study. Related enemies, such as a weak and a normal zombie, or all Hiisi, are faster to study too, but less so.
- At first, an enemy takes the `Base study time` to study. Once you know its type well, it takes only half a second.
- Bosses always take two minutes to study, and studying them teaches you nothing about other enemies.
